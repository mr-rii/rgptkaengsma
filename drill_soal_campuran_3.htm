Text 1

Coral reefs are some of the most colorful and diverse ecosystems on Earth. Although they cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, they are home to more than twenty-five percent of all marine species. Corals are actually small animals called polyps that live together in large colonies. They build hard limestone skeletons that form giant underwater structures over thousands of years.

Coral reefs provide crucial benefits to marine animals and human communities. They offer food and shelter for thousands of fish, turtles, and sea creatures. For humans, coral reefs protect coastlines from storm waves, preventing severe erosion near coastal towns. In addition, millions of people depend on coral reefs for fishing and tourism, which brings income to local economies.

Unfortunately, coral reefs are facing serious threats today. Ocean warming caused by global climate change leads to coral bleaching. When ocean water becomes too warm, corals lose the colorful algae living inside them and turn completely white. If the water stays hot for too long, the corals die. Pollution from plastic waste and farm chemicals also harms these delicate ecosystems.

To save coral reefs, scientists and governments are taking action. They are creating marine protected areas where fishing is restricted. Additionally, volunteers are planting nursery-grown corals back onto damaged reefs. Protecting these valuable ecosystems is essential to keep our oceans healthy for future generations.

1. What is the main idea of the text?

Explanation: The text describes what coral reefs are, their benefits, environmental threats, and protection efforts.

2. What is the primary purpose of the text?

Explanation: The primary goal is to describe coral reefs, their importance, threats, and conservation.

3. The word "crucial" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to...

Explanation: The word "crucial" means extremely important or vital.

4. The word "They" in paragraph 1 ("They build hard limestone skeletons...") refers to...

Explanation: The word "They" refers back to "small animals called polyps".

Text 2

Clean water is essential for human health and survival. In many science classes, students learn how natural filtration systems clean dirty water. Building a simple water filter using everyday materials is a great way to understand how soil and sand filter rainwater in nature. Here is a step-by-step guide to making a basic water filter in a school laboratory.

First, take a clean plastic bottle and cut it in half using scissors. Turn the top half upside down so it looks like a funnel, and place it inside the bottom half of the bottle. Second, put a small piece of cotton or a coffee filter into the neck of the bottle. This initial layer prevents sand and charcoal from falling through the opening into the clean water container.

Third, add a layer of crushed activated charcoal on top of the cotton. Charcoal is very important because it absorbs tiny impurities and removes unpleasant odors from the dirty liquid. Fourth, add a thick layer of fine sand over the charcoal, followed by a layer of coarse gravel at the top. The gravel traps large debris like leaves and twigs, while the sand catches smaller dirt particles.

Finally, pour muddy water slowly into the top of the filter. Watch as the dirty liquid passes through each layer. The water coming out at the bottom will look much clearer. Remember, this simple lab filter removes visible dirt, but it does not kill bacteria. Therefore, you must boil the filtered water before drinking it.

5. What is the primary purpose of the text?

Explanation: The text gives step-by-step instructions on how to make a simple water filter in a lab.

6. Who would benefit the most from reading this text? (Check all that apply)

Explanation: Science students, teachers doing lab activities, and campers needing clean water benefit most.

7. The word "essential" in paragraph 1 is closest in meaning to...

Explanation: The word "essential" means extremely necessary.

8. The word "it" in paragraph 3 ("...because it absorbs tiny impurities...") refers to...

Explanation: The word "it" refers to crushed activated charcoal which absorbs impurities.

Come and sing with me instead!"

The Ant stopped for a moment and replied, "I am storing food for the cold winter months. I advise you to do the same, my friend, because winter will arrive sooner than you think." However, the Grasshopper ignored her advice. "Winter is still far away, and there is plenty of green leaves to eat right now," he cheered, continuing to dance throughout the warm summer and autumn.

When winter finally arrived, freezing winds blew across the forest, and deep snow covered all the plants and trees. The hungry Grasshopper searched everywhere for food, but he found nothing to eat. Weak and shivering from the cold, he remembered the hardworking Ant and crawled to her warm underground home to beg for help.

The Ant opened her door and saw the starving Grasshopper. She felt pity for him and invited him inside to share her warm meal. While eating, the Grasshopper realized his mistake and promised never to be lazy again. From that day on, he learned that working hard today prepares us for the difficulties of tomorrow.

Text 4

Bacharuddin Jusuf Habibie, widely known as B.J. Habibie, was the third President of Indonesia and a brilliant aerospace engineer. Born on June 25, 1936, in Parepare, South Sulawesi, Habibie showed a passionate interest in science and technology from a very young age. After completing his high school education in Bandung, he traveled to Germany to study aeronautical engineering at the prestigious RWTH Aachen University.

Habibie excelled in his academic studies through incredible determination and hard work. He earned his doctorate degree with top honors and worked for several prominent German aircraft companies. During his time in Europe, he developed a famous scientific theory regarding aircraft structure known as the "Habibie Factor," which calculated crack progression in airplane wings. His groundbreaking discovery earned him international recognition in the global aviation industry.

In 1974, President Soeharto invited Habibie to return to Indonesia to help build the nation's technology sector. Habibie led the state-owned aircraft company IPTN and successfully designed the N-250 Gatotkaca, the first regional passenger aircraft built entirely by Indonesian engineers. Later, during a major economic crisis in 1998, Habibie assumed the presidency and successfully led the country through a peaceful democratic transition.

Throughout his life, Habibie demonstrated extraordinary intelligence, patriotism, and humility. He dedicated his life to proving that Indonesian minds could compete on the world stage of science and innovation. Even after retiring from politics, he remained a beloved figure who inspired millions of young Indonesians to pursue education and technological excellence.

Text 5

Traffic congestion is a major problem in many modern growing cities around the world. Every morning, millions of private cars and motorcycles crowd city streets, causing severe traffic jams and air pollution. To solve this urban crisis, city governments must invest more resources in expanding public transportation systems, such as buses, trains, and subways.

First, public transportation significantly reduces traffic congestion on urban roads. A single passenger bus or train can carry dozens or even hundreds of commuters at once. If more citizens choose to ride public transit instead of driving private vehicles, the total number of cars on the road will decrease dramatically. Consequently, daily travel times will become faster and safer for everyone.

Second, public transit helps protect the environment by cutting carbon emissions. Private vehicles release large amounts of harmful exhaust gases that pollute city air and contribute to global climate change. In contrast, modern electric trains and hybrid buses produce far fewer emissions per passenger kilometer. Furthermore, using public transport saves citizens money on fuel, parking fees, and vehicle maintenance.

In conclusion, developing a strong public transportation network is essential for sustainable city living. Governments should make public transit affordable, comfortable, and reliable so that citizens feel encouraged to leave their private cars at home.

17. Which statement best summarizes the text?

Explanation: Expanding public transportation reduces traffic congestion, cuts air pollution, and saves money.
