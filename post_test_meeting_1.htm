Exploring Makassar: A City of Sea and History

Makassar is a busy port city on the island of Sulawesi in Indonesia. It is famous for its long history, delicious food, and beautiful seaside views. For tourists, Makassar offers a wonderful mix of nature, city life, and culture. Here are three iconic places you must visit.

First, you cannot miss Losari Beach. This is not a beach for swimming, but it is the heart of the city. Every evening, hundreds of people come here to walk, talk, and enjoy the famous sunset. The view of the sea is calm and beautiful. Across the street, you can find many food stalls selling local Makassar dishes. It is the perfect place to feel the energy of the city.

For nature lovers, Samalona Island is a short boat trip from the city. The water around this small island is very clear and blue. You can swim, snorkel to see colourful fish, or simply relax on the white sand. It feels like a peaceful escape from the busy city. Remember to bring your sunscreen and a hat!

Finally, to understand the history of this region, visit Fort Rotterdam. This is a large, old fort built from dark stone. It was very important for trade hundreds of years ago. Now, it is a museum. You can walk inside the strong walls, see old rooms, and learn about local history and culture. It is a quiet and interesting place that tells the story of Makassar's past.

In conclusion, Makassar is a fascinating destination. You can enjoy a modern city sunset at Losari, discover underwater beauty at Samalona, and walk through history at Fort Rotterdam. It is a trip you will always remember.

A Lost Friendship

I want to tell you about one of my saddest experiences from when I was fifteen years old. It wasn’t a big disaster, but it was the slow end of my closest friendship.

My best friend's name was Leo. We did everything together since primary school. We played football every afternoon, we liked the same video games, and we even helped each other with homework. I thought our friendship would last forever.

The problem started when we went to different high schools. At first, we called each other every weekend and met every month. But slowly, our lives became different. I joined the school band and made new friends there. Leo joined the basketball team and was always busy with practice. We started to call less often. When we did talk, the conversations felt short and a little awkward. We didn’t have the same daily experiences to share anymore.

One day, I saw a photo online. It was Leo with his new basketball friends on a trip I didn’t know about. That was the moment I felt a deep sadness. I realised we were already living separate lives. I sent him a message, "Looks like a fun trip!" He replied quickly, "Yeah, it was great! We should catch up soon." But we both knew that "soon" would probably never come. We never had a big fight or argument. The friendship just faded away quietly, like a song ending.

Even now, many years later, I still feel a little sad when I remember Leo. I learned that some friendships are for a season of your life. You can be very close for a time, and then your paths simply go in different directions. It’s a natural part of growing up, but it still hurts.

Becoming a Politically Aware Person

Being politically aware doesn’t mean you have to argue about complicated laws all the time. It simply means understanding how decisions are made in your community and country, and how they affect your life. As a teenager, you can start this journey with small, manageable steps. Here is how you can begin.

First, find reliable sources of information. This is the most important step. Do not believe everything you see on social media. Follow a few trusted news websites, newspapers, or TV channels. Start with just 10 minutes a day. Read headlines or watch one short news summary. Good sources give facts, not just strong opinions.

Next, talk and listen to people around you. Have calm conversations with your family, teachers, or friends about things happening in your city or school. Ask questions like, "What do you think about this new rule?" or "How does this problem affect us?" Listen carefully to different opinions. This helps you see one issue from many sides.

Then, connect politics to your own life. Politics isn’t just about big leaders in a faraway capital. Think about your school. Are there rules you think are unfair? Think about your town. Is the park clean? Is public transport good? These are all local political issues. Understanding this connection makes politics real and interesting for you.

Finally, take one small action. Awareness leads to action. You don’t need to start a big protest. You can write a respectful email to your school council about an idea you have. You can join a school club that focuses on the environment or community service. You can even just vote in a student election. These actions teach you how change happens.

Remember, becoming politically aware is a process. Start small, stay curious, and use your voice respectfully. Your perspective as a young person is important for the future.

The Legend of Banyuwangi

Long ago, the beautiful eastern tip of Java was ruled by a king named Prabu Sulahkromo. He was assisted by a brave, wise, and handsome Prime Minister named Patih Sidopekso. The Patih’s wife, Sri Tanjung, was so beautiful and kind that the king fell madly in love with her.

The king wanted to be alone with Sri Tanjung, so he thought of a clever trick. He sent Patih Sidopekso on an impossible mission. The loyal Patih left immediately, not suspecting anything. As soon as he was gone, the king tried to win Sri Tanjung’s love with sweet words and lies. But she remained faithful, thinking only of her husband. The king was furious at her refusal.

When Patih Sidopekso returned, he reported to the king. The king then told a terrible lie. He said that while the Patih was away, Sri Tanjung had tried to seduce him.

Blind with anger, Patih Sidopekso rushed home. He shouted terrible accusations at his wife. Her honest explanation only made him angrier. He threatened to kill her. He dragged Sri Tanjung to a muddy river.

Before he could strike, Sri Tanjung made one last request. "Kill me," she said, "and throw my body into the river. If my blood makes the water smell foul, I was guilty. But if it smells sweet, you will know I was always faithful."

The Patih, lost in rage, stabbed his wife. Her body fell into the dirty river. Then, a miracle happened. The water became clear as glass and filled with a wonderful, sweet fragrance. Patih Sidopekso fell to his knees, heartbroken. Over and over, he cried out in sorrow, "Banyu wangi... Banyu wangi..." which means "fragrant water." And that is how the place got its name: Banyuwangi, born from the proof of a wife’s true love for her husband.

TEXT 5: ANALYTICAL EXPOSITION Soal 21 - 25

Social Media Detox

In today’s world, social media is a big part of our lives. However, using it too much can cause problems. I believe that regularly doing a "social media detox"—taking a break from apps like Instagram or TikTok—is very important for our mental health and real-life happiness.

First, constant social media use can make us feel stressed and inadequate. We often see perfect photos of vacations, bodies, and happy moments from other people. This can make us compare our normal lives to these highlights. This comparison creates pressure and can lower our self-esteem. A detox gives our mind a rest from this unhealthy comparison, allowing us to focus on our own progress.

Second, social media consumes a lot of our precious time. We can spend hours scrolling without realizing it. This is time we could use for hobbies, exercise, meeting friends face-to-face, or simply relaxing with a book. A detox helps us break this automatic habit and gives us back control of our time. We rediscover activities that bring us real joy and connection.

Finally, a detox improves our real-world relationships. When we are not checking our phones every minute, we can listen better and be more present with the people in front of us. Our conversations become deeper and more meaningful.

In conclusion, a social media detox is not about deleting accounts forever. It is a short, intentional break to reset our minds, reclaim our time, and reconnect with the real world. It is a simple but vital practice for a healthier and happier life.
