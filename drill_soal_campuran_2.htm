Text 2

Testing soil pH is a simple and important science lab experiment. The pH level tells us if soil is acidic, neutral, or alkaline. Plants need the right soil pH to absorb nutrients and grow healthy. By following these steps, students can easily measure soil pH in a school laboratory.

First, collect a small soil sample from a garden or field. Place the soil on a clean paper towel to remove small stones, leaves, and twigs. Second, put two spoonfuls of the clean soil into a clean glass jar. Add four spoonfuls of distilled water into the jar. Do not use tap water because tap water contains minerals that can change the pH test result.

Third, stir the mixture gently with a clean spoon for one minute. Let the glass jar sit untouched for 10 minutes so the soil can settle at the bottom and the water becomes clear. Fourth, take a piece of pH indicator paper strip. Dip the tip of the pH strip into the clear liquid at the top of the jar for two seconds.

Finally, remove the strip and wait 30 seconds for the color to change. Compare the color on the strip with the official pH color chart provided in your lab kit. A pH value of 7 means neutral soil, below 7 means acidic soil, and above 7 means alkaline soil. Record your result in your science notebook.

5. What is the primary purpose of the text?

Explanation: The main goal of the text is to give step-by-step instructions on how to test soil pH in a school lab.

3. _________________________________
4. Dip pH indicator paper into the clear liquid.
5. Match strip color with the chart and record pH value.

Explanation: Step 3 is stirring the mixture and letting it sit for 10 minutes so the water becomes clear.

7. Based on the text, what will most likely happen if a student uses tap water instead of distilled water?

Explanation: Tap water has minerals. This can change the result and give a wrong pH number.

8. Which step in the text best supports the idea that cleanliness is necessary for accurate test results?

Explanation: Cleaning the soil from stones and leaves on a clean paper towel shows that cleanliness is very important for the test.

Text 3

Ki Hajar Dewantara was a great Indonesian national hero and the pioneer of education in Indonesia. He was born on May 2, 1889, in Yogyakarta with the name Raden Mas Soewardi Soerjaningrat. Coming from a noble family, he had the chance to attend school, which was a rare privilege for most Indonesian children during the Dutch colonial era.

When he grew up, Soewardi worked as a writer and journalist for several newspapers. He used his writing to criticize the unfair Dutch colonial government. In 1913, he wrote a famous article titled "Als ik eens Nederlander was" (If I Were a Netherlander), which strongly protested Dutch celebrations paid for by Indonesian people. Because of his brave writings, the Dutch government exiled him to the Netherlands.

During his exile in Europe, Soewardi studied education concepts deeply. When he returned to Indonesia in 1919, he changed his name to Ki Hajar Dewantara so he could be closer to common people. On July 3, 1922, he founded Taman Siswa in Yogyakarta. This school allowed ordinary Indonesian children to get a good education, not just children from noble families.

Ki Hajar Dewantara introduced the famous educational motto: "Ing Ngarsa Sung Tuladha, Ing Madya Mangun Karsa, Tut Wuri Handayani" (In front giving example, in the middle building spirit, behind giving encouragement). After Indonesia gained independence, he was appointed as the nation's first Minister of Education. His birthday, May 2, is celebrated every year as National Education Day.

9. What is the main idea of the text?

Explanation: The main idea is that Ki Hajar Dewantara was a national hero who fought for equal education and built Taman Siswa.

3. Being appointed as Indonesia's first Minister of Education
4. Being exiled to the Netherlands by the Dutch government
5. Celebrating his birthday as National Education Day every year
Explanation: Items 1, 2, and 4 happened before 1945 (1913 and 1922). Items 3 and 5 happened after Indonesia became independent.

11. Which of the following can be inferred from the text?

Explanation: He wanted education for everyone, changed his name to be close to common people, and his ideas still inspire us today.

Explanation: The best action for a student is to study hard and respect equal education for everyone.

Text 4

In a large forest, a proud Hare loved to boast about his speed. "No one in this forest can run as fast as me!" he boasted to the other animals. Tired of his bragging, a slow Tortoise calmly challenged the Hare to a three-kilometer race through the woods. The Hare laughed loudly and accepted, thinking the race would be very easy to win.

The next morning, the race began. The Hare ran very fast and soon left the Tortoise far behind. Seeing how slow the Tortoise was moving, the Hare decided to stop under a shady oak tree. "I have plenty of time," the Hare thought to himself. "I will take a short nap, wake up, and still win easily." He closed his eyes and fell fast asleep.

Meanwhile, the Tortoise kept walking step by step. He did not stop, and he did not complain about being tired. Slowly but steadily, he passed the sleeping Hare. Hours passed, and the sun began to set.

When the Hare finally woke up, he looked down the trail and saw the Tortoise just a few inches away from the finish line. The Hare jumped up and ran as fast as his legs could carry him, but it was too late. The Tortoise crossed the line first and won the race. All the forest animals cheered for the Tortoise's quiet determination.

13. What is the main idea of the text?

Explanation: The proud Hare lost the race to the steady Tortoise because he was too confident and fell asleep.

14. According to the text, which of the following details are true?

Explanation: The text states the Hare boasted, the Tortoise challenged him, the Hare slept under a tree, and the Tortoise finished first without resting.

15. Classify whether each personality trait below describes the Hare or the Tortoise.

Trait Hare Tortoise
1. Overconfident and boastful
2. Patient and persistent
3. Lazy during the race
4. Calm and humble
5. Determined not to give up
Explanation: The Hare was overconfident and lazy. The Tortoise was patient, calm, and determined.

16. What moral lesson can we learn from the story?

Explanation: The story teaches that slow and steady effort wins. Being too proud and lazy leads to losing.

Text 5

Getting enough sleep is very important for high school students. Many teenagers stay up late playing video games, watching television, or scrolling on smartphones. However, sleeping 8 to 10 hours every night is essential for good health, mental focus, and academic success.

First, adequate sleep improves brain function and memory. When students sleep well, their brain organizes information learned during the day. This process helps students remember lessons better when taking exams at school. Conversely, tired students often struggle to concentrate in class and forget important facts easily.

Second, sufficient sleep supports physical health and emotional well-being. Sleep helps the body repair muscles and strengthens the immune system to fight off illness. Furthermore, well-rested students feel happier and less stressed, making it easier to handle daily school tasks and socialize with friends.

In conclusion, sleep is not a waste of time; it is a vital necessity for young people. Students should set a regular bedtime and turn off electronic devices early to ensure a good night's rest for better learning and living.

17. What is the main purpose of the text?

Explanation: The text wants to persuade readers that getting enough sleep is very important for high school students.

18. According to the text, what are the benefits of getting enough sleep?

Explanation: Sleep improves brain function, makes the immune system strong, and helps students feel less stressed.

19. What is the relationship between Paragraph 2 and Paragraph 3?

Explanation: Paragraph 3 gives a second supporting reason (physical and emotional health). It follows the first reason in Paragraph 2 (brain and memory).
