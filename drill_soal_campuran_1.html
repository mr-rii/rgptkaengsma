TEXT 1: Descriptive Text (Geography) Soal 1 - 4

The Sahara Desert is one of the most extreme environments on Earth, yet it features a fascinating geography filled with diverse physical landforms. Covering a massive area across North Africa, the desert is not merely an endless ocean of flat sand dunes, known locally as erg, which account for only a small portion of the region. A significant part of the Sahara actually consists of rocky plateaus called hamadas, gravel plains known as regs, and dry valleys called wadis that only experience water flow during rare flash floods.

Climate conditions in the Sahara are exceptionally harsh. Daytime temperatures frequently exceed 40 degrees Celsius during the summer months due to direct sunlight and very low humidity. Conversely, because the dry air cannot trap heat effectively, nighttime temperatures can drop drastically, sometimes falling close to freezing point in the winter. Rainfall is extremely scarce across the entire region, with some areas going years without seeing a single drop of precipitation.

Despite these punishing conditions, various plants and animals have adapted remarkably well to survive. Vegetation is largely restricted to desert oases, dry riverbeds, and mountainous high-altitude areas where moisture collects. Native fauna includes dromedary camels, fennec foxes, and various species of reptiles that can endure extreme heat and survive for long periods without drinking water.

TEXT 2: Procedure Text (Geography) Soal 5 - 8

Measuring the flow rate of a local river is an essential hydrological field technique used by geographers to understand watershed dynamics. Conducting this procedure accurately requires careful preparation, specific safety measures, and reliable measuring tools. By following a structured step-by-step method, students can calculate the volume of water passing through a specific channel per second.

To begin the process, select a safe, straight section of the river where the water flows evenly without turbulent rapids or deep pools. First, measure a standard distance along the riverbank, typically ten meters, and mark the starting and ending points clearly using flags or wooden stakes. Second, measure the cross-sectional area of the channel by taking depth readings at regular intervals across the width of the river, then multiplying the average depth by the total width.

Third, calculate the surface velocity of the water. Drop a floating object, such as a small wooden stick or an orange, into the water at the starting marker and record the exact number of seconds it takes to travel the ten-meter distance. Repeat this float test at least three times across different parts of the channel to find the average travel time, then divide the distance by the average time to determine water velocity.

Finally, multiply the cross-sectional area by the average velocity and apply a correction factor of 0.85, because water near the riverbed and banks moves slower than surface water due to friction. Recording these calculations carefully allows researchers to determine the total discharge volume accurately. Always ensure a partner is present on the riverbank to maintain safety throughout the entire procedure.

TEXT 3: Biography Recount Text (Sociology) Soal 9 - 12

Dr. Elena Vance was a renowned contemporary sociologist whose groundbreaking research transformed our understanding of urban poverty and social inequality in modern metropolitan cities. Born in a small industrial town in 1975, Elena developed an early fascination with how physical neighborhood structures influence human behavior and community relationships. She pursued her academic passion at university, earning a doctorate in sociology from a prestigious institute before beginning her lifelong career in academic research and social advocacy.

During her early professional years, Dr. Vance spent five immersive years living inside low-income housing communities across three major cities. Rather than relying solely on traditional statistical surveys, she utilized qualitative field research methods, including deep participant observation and open-ended interviews. Her meticulous findings revealed how systemic economic barriers trapped vulnerable families in cycles of generational poverty, while simultaneously highlighting the remarkable resilience and informal mutual aid networks forged by local residents.

In 2010, Dr. Vance published her seminal book, Shadows of the City, which quickly became required reading in university sociology departments worldwide. The publication challenged prevailing political narratives that blamed individuals for their economic struggles, shifting public attention toward structural policy failures instead. Following the book's massive success, she established an urban research foundation dedicated to advising local governments on fair housing policies and equitable community development initiatives.

Dr. Vance continued teaching, writing, and advocating for marginalized populations until her retirement in 2022. Although she passed away peacefully last year, her visionary scholarship and compassionate approach to sociological inquiry continue to inspire new generations of social scientists striving to build fairer, more inclusive urban societies.

TEXT 4: Narrative Text (Pinocchio) Soal 13 - 16

Once upon a time, in a cozy Italian workshop, an old woodcarver named Geppetto carved a wonderful wooden puppet and named him Pinocchio. That very night, a kind blue fairy visited the workshop and magically brought the wooden puppet to life, promising that if he proved to be brave, truthful, and unselfish, he would one day become a real flesh-and-blood boy. Geppetto was overjoyed when he woke up and discovered his creation could walk and talk.

Eager to learn about the world, Pinocchio was sent off to school with a spelling book. However, along the way, he was easily distracted by mischief and temptation. He allowed cunning characters like a deceitful fox and a lazy cat to lead him astray into the Land of Toys, a magical playground where children played games all day without studying or working. Ignoring the wise warnings of his conscience, a small talking cricket, Pinocchio wasted precious days playing foolish games.

Consequences soon followed his disobedience. As punishment for his poor choices and lies, Pinocchio suddenly sprouted a pair of long donkey ears and grew a long wooden nose that stretched further every time he told a falsehood. Filled with deep remorse for breaking his father's heart, Pinocchio finally realized his severe mistakes. He resolved to change his ways, escaped from the trap, and set out across the dangerous stormy sea to rescue Geppetto, who had been swallowed by a giant whale while searching for him.

Inside the dark belly of the whale, Pinocchio bravely rescued his aging father by building a smoky fire that forced the creature to sneeze them out. Washing ashore safely, Pinocchio collapsed from exhaustion after shielding his father from harm. Touched by his ultimate sacrifice, selfless courage, and honesty, the blue fairy transformed the wooden puppet into a real, living boy. Geppetto wept tears of absolute joy, and they lived happily together ever after.

TEXT 5: Analytical Exposition (Economics) Soal 17 - 20

Inflation is one of the most critical macroeconomic indicators affecting modern national economies. Defined as the general increase in prices and the subsequent fall in the purchasing value of money over time, inflation directly impacts household stability, business investments, and government fiscal policies. While moderate inflation is often considered a normal sign of a growing economy, runaway or hyperinflation can severely destabilize financial systems and impoverish working-class citizens.

There are two primary drivers of inflation: demand-pull inflation and cost-push inflation. Demand-pull inflation occurs when aggregate demand for goods and services outpaces aggregate supply, causing sellers to raise prices due to high consumer appetite. Conversely, cost-push inflation happens when the costs of production—such as raw materials, energy, and labor wages—increase significantly, forcing businesses to pass those extra expenses onto consumers. Both mechanisms reduce the real value of savings, making it harder for fixed-income families to afford basic daily necessities.

Governments and central banks utilize monetary and fiscal policies to control excessive price increases. Central banks frequently adjust benchmark interest rates; raising interest rates cools down consumer spending and borrowing, which successfully curbs demand-pull pressures. Additionally, governments can improve supply chain efficiencies and subsidize essential production sectors to counteract cost-push inflation.

In conclusion, understanding inflation is vital for maintaining economic health. Without active intervention and balanced monetary policies, unchecked inflation can destroy market confidence, erode citizen wealth, and trigger severe economic recessions.
